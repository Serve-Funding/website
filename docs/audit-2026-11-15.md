## 1. Summary

- **Website.** It is thorough (about 150,000 words) and technically sound for AI search. Crawlers are allowed in, IndexNow is wired, dates come from git, and pages answer the question first. The weak spot is proof. Several headline numbers are contradicted by the CRM or cannot be supported: "100+ clients", "65% repeat clients", "funded in 3-10 days", "average deal $500K-$3.35MM", "100% of referred deals funded" and "10-20 percentage points cheaper". The same figures go to AI engines through `llms-full.txt` and the chatbot.
- **The home page** opens on three rotating slogans. It never says plainly that Serve is an advisor, not a lender, and that it helps when the bank says no. The real proof sits on deep pages: candour, "when this is the wrong answer", 23 case studies and success-fee-only terms.
- **Portal copy.** It is mostly plain and accurate. Four client-facing lines are wrong today. The invite email promises an engagement-agreement step the client no longer sees. The founder letter says "we only ask you to sign once we have lenders", but the application is signed before submit. An unenforced "within one business day" promise. "AI-native" in the lender emails. No template signs off the way Mike does.
- **Voice.** The team's voice profile was built from about 30 emails in one week. Checked against 400 recent emails and 900 call lines, it gets the core right: warm, specific, "Here to serve," (in 362 of 400 emails). But it has the greetings wrong, lists phrases he no longer uses, and has no spoken-voice section.
- **The five changes that matter most:**
  1. Remove or replace every unsupported number in one pass (`src/data/company-info.ts`, `src/data/faq-data.ts`, page metadata, both llms files).
  2. Rewrite the top of the home page: one fixed H1 that says what Serve is, then a proof strip the CRM supports.
  3. Put the front-door candour on `/discover` and in the FAQ: what happens after you submit, no commitment until we show you a path, who we are not a fit for.
  4. Fix the four wrong lines in the portal's client and lender emails, and sign every template "Here to serve," plus a person's name.
  5. Replace the email section of Mike's voice profile and add a spoken-voice section, so AI drafts and new copy stop copying phrases he doesn't use.

## 4. Mike's voice

### Corrected profile, in brief

Sources: 400 sent emails (15 Sep to 13 Nov 2026; 312 after removing duplicate drafts) and 900 of his longer call lines (Jan to Nov 2026). The old profile came from about 30 emails in one week of March to April.

**Email.**
- Calm, specific, polite. Typical length is 40-100 words; a quarter are under 30.
- Opens by thanking the person for one concrete thing, or with the news itself.
- Says what happens next and why it helps the reader. Asks for one clear thing.
- Ends "Thank you," then "Here to serve," on its own line (362 of 400 emails).
- Greetings: "Hi Name," for people he knows (the most common). "Good afternoon Name," for lenders, new contacts and long emails. A bare "Name," in threads. "Good morning" is the least common time-based greeting, not the most common as the profile says. "Dear" is nearly gone.
- Words he really uses: "data room" (42 emails), "executive summary", "term sheet", "stips", "go to market", "circle back" (19 emails, which the profile says he avoids), "stay tuned", "run point", "simple math", "far from ideal", "would you please", "grab a time".
- Phrases the profile lists that do not appear in recent mail: "full speed ahead", "unlock liquidity", "heavy lifting", "out of the box", "tear sheet", "Howdy", "awesome". "Sweet spot" appears once.
- Bad news: the plain reason, with the real constraint, and no blame. Then what is still possible. A mistake is owned in one sentence.
- With referrers he always gives the reason for a no and restates what Serve does want.
- About 32 emails (8%) are long, uniformly laid out and HTML-escaped. They look composed in a tool, possibly the portal composer **[unsure]**. Do not learn his voice from them.

**Spoken (new section).**
- Explains every product the same way: one plain sentence, a picture (insurance broker, triangle, spectrum from bankable to "hairy"), who it suits, the cost as a range with "typically" or "rule of thumb", what the lender will want, and an under-promised timing.
- Always caveats before a number ("not being a lawyer…").
- Names what he won't do: he won't promise, won't oversize a facility, won't get your hopes up.
- Offers the exit: "you can fire us at any time".
- Tells owners how lenders think ("they all start with, what's the risk") and asks for the bad news early ("honest, real, but strategic").
- Says "broker" as readily as "advisor".
- Fillers ("you know" in 268 lines) stay out of copy. Markers that carry meaning stay: "simply put", "to be clear", "look,".

**Limits.** The emails cover two months only. There are just 2 internal emails, so his voice to the team is unchecked. The call file holds only his long turns, so how he qualifies a deal is under-represented.

### Do

1. Thank the person for one specific thing first.
2. "Hi Name," for known people; "Good afternoon/morning Name," for lenders, new contacts and long messages.
3. Say what happens next and why it helps them.
4. Ask for one clear thing. Use hyphen bullets for documents and numbers for questions.
5. Name dates, amounts and terms exactly as they are in the file.
6. Offer two or three times, or the calendar link.
7. Give bad news straight away, with the real reason, then what is still possible.
8. Put the weak parts of a deal in front of a lender early.
9. Thank referrers every time, even when the answer is no.
10. Close with "Thank you," then "Here to serve," and a person's name.
11. Keep paragraphs to 1-3 sentences.
12. Own a mistake in one sentence.
13. Name the team member who does the next step.
14. In copy that should sound like him speaking: a plain sentence, a picture, a range with a caveat.

### Don't

1. "Dear", "Best regards", "Hope this finds you well", "per my last email", "just a friendly nudge", "touch base".
2. Emojis, slang, ALL CAPS for emphasis (acronyms and a "DATA ROOM:" label are fine), "#HereToServe" in email (it is a social stamp).
3. Profile phrases he no longer uses: "sweet spot", "full speed ahead", "unlock liquidity", "heavy lifting", "Howdy", "awesome".
4. Blame anyone.
5. Promise an approval, a rate, a speed or a saving. "10-20 points cheaper" is not his.
6. Hype words: "AI-native", "seamless", "streamline", "game-changing".
7. False deadlines. State real ones plainly.
8. Pad. A one-line answer gets a one-line email.
9. Invent client facts or lender names.
10. Copy the long "composed" style as if it were his typing.
11. Use "turn-down" in email on the strength of Section IX alone.

## 5. GEO / AIEO: what current practice says and where the site stands

### What the evidence says

The platforms' own statements are the firmest evidence. The studies are mostly vendor research and show correlation, not cause.

- **Google: "AI search is still SEO."** A page needs to be indexed and eligible for a snippet. Google rewards non-commodity content with first-hand experience. It says llms.txt, chunking content, "GEO hacks" and special AI markup are not needed, and that mass pages for query variations can fall under its scaled-content spam policy. The guide was published around May 2026. Sources: [Google AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide), [AI features and your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features). I confirmed this through secondary write-ups; for example, [Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/blog/google-publishes-generative-ai-search-guide/) and [Practical Ecommerce](https://practicalecommerce.com/google-says-ai-optimization-is-just-seo) both say Google lists llms.txt among tactics with no special advantage. I did not open the Google page itself.
- **Bing** added GEO to its Webmaster Guidelines in February 2026, along with rules against engineered language and prompt injection. It also launched an AI Performance report showing Copilot citations by page. Sources: [SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/bing-adds-geo-to-official-guidelines-expands-ai-abuse-definitions/), [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/bing-webmaster-tools-testing-new-ai-performance-report-468039).
- **Crawlers.** OpenAI's OAI-SearchBot decides whether a site appears in ChatGPT search; GPTBot is for training only ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots)). Anthropic splits ClaudeBot, Claude-User and Claude-SearchBot ([Anthropic](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8896518)). Perplexity: [PerplexityBot docs](https://docs.perplexity.ai/guides/perplexitybot).
- **Off-site mentions matter most (correlation).** In Ahrefs' 75,000-brand study, YouTube mentions correlated most strongly with AI visibility (about 0.74), then branded web mentions (0.66-0.71). Backlinks were weak. Caveat: secondary coverage says the sample was filtered to larger sites (DR above 40), so it may apply less to a firm Serve's size. Sources: [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-brand-visibility-correlations/), [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/ahrefs-youtube-mentions-ai-visibility-brand-search).
- **Statistics, cited sources and quotations** raised visibility by up to about 40% in the Princeton GEO paper. That was a 2023-24 test set, not today's engines ([arXiv 2311.09735](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735)).
- **llms.txt** showed no correlation with citations across about 300,000 domains ([SE Ranking](https://seranking.com/blog/llms-txt/)).
- **FAQ markup** no longer earns rich results, and claims that it boosts AI visibility are unproven ([Quattr](https://www.quattr.com/blog/faq-schema-in-2026), vendor).
- **Freshness.** Real updates help; date bumps do not clearly pay ([Seer Interactive](https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/study-ai-brand-visibility-and-content-recency)).
- **Self-promotional "best X" lists** get cited, but AI Overviews often cite the list and then recommend a competitor ([Peec](https://peec.ai/blog/self-promotional-listicles-analysis-from-232k-citations)).
- **Self-reviews** do not get review stars in schema ([Google, 2019](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2019/09/making-review-rich-results-more-helpful)).
- **Wasted effort or spam:** a page per query variant, thin AI-written volume, keyword stuffing, hidden instructions aimed at models, fake mentions or reviews, date bumping, chunk-size rules.

### Where the site stands

**Doing well:**
- `public/robots.txt` allows every major AI crawler, including OAI-SearchBot.
- IndexNow runs on every push to main (`.github/workflows/indexnow.yml`).
- Sitemap dates come from git.
- Comparison and solution pages answer first and include an honest "when this is the wrong answer".
- Mike's own words appear across the pages.
- No self-review markup ships: `getReviewSchema` exists but is unused.
- Blog dates are honest.

**Gaps, in order of impact:**
1. **Our numbers disagree** with each other and with the CRM. They reach AI engines through `llms-full.txt` and the chatbot. Fix: Q2-Q9, M1, M10.
2. **Almost no off-site footprint.** `sameAs` lists only LinkedIn and Facebook; there are 2 videos and no channel. Fix: L2, L3.
3. **First-party data is unpublished.** Fix: L1.
4. **Few primary sources are linked.** The Fed Small Business Credit Survey is named in two posts but not linked. Add a short "Sources" line to posts and product pages, and mark which numbers are Serve's own experience. Medium effort; fold into M6 and M9.
5. **Freshness is per file, not per page.** Fix: M9.
6. **The content plan pushes volume.** Fix: L6.
7. **Nothing is measured.** Fix: M11.
8. **Schema entities are not linked.** Organization and founder have no stable `@id`, and authors are inline (`src/lib/schema-generators.ts:221-265`). This is hygiene, not a lever.
